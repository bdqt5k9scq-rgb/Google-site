What is a Carbon Footprint?

Definition of Carbon Footprint

Carbon Footprint is the total amount of greenhouse gases emitted directly and indirectly by an individual, organization, activity, or product throughout its entire life cycle, typically expressed in carbon dioxide equivalent (CO₂e).

The concept of carbon footprint originates from the ecological footprint theory, proposed by Canadian environmental scientists William Rees and Mathis Wackernagel in 1992. It helps us quantify the impact of human activities on climate change.

Why Is It Important?

Climate change is one of the greatest challenges facing humanity in the 21st century. The global average temperature has already risen by approximately 1.1°C compared to pre-industrial levels.

Connection to the SDGs:

• SDG 13 (Climate Action): Take urgent action to combat climate change
• SDG 7 (Clean Energy): Ensure sustainable modern energy
• SDG 12 (Responsible Consumption): Ensure sustainable consumption patterns

Current Carbon Emission Status

According to the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) 2023 report:

• Global average annual carbon footprint per capita: approx. 4.7 tonnes CO₂e
• Developed countries can reach 15-20 tonnes per capita
• Global annual CO₂ emissions: approx. 36 billion tonnes

Emission Reduction Targets

The Paris Agreement target: limit global temperature rise to well below 2°C, and pursue efforts to limit it to 1.5°C.

To achieve this target, global carbon emissions must be reduced by 43% by 2030, and net-zero emissions must be achieved by 2050.
