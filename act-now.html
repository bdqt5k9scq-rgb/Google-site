Li Shuhang

SDG 7 · Save Household Energy

Step 1: Purpose of the Action

Household energy consumption accounts for approximately 30% of global greenhouse gas emissions and is one of the largest components of personal carbon footprint. In China, a typical urban household produces approximately 2,500 to 4,000 kg of CO₂ annually from electricity consumption, mainly from lighting, air conditioning, household appliance standby power, and heating. Many households are not yet aware that simple behavioral changes — such as replacing incandescent bulbs with LEDs, unplugging unused electrical appliances, and setting air conditioners to appropriate temperatures — can significantly reduce energy consumption and carbon emissions. The purpose of this action is to systematically assess household energy use, identify high-consumption devices and poor electricity habits, develop actionable energy-saving plans, and quantify personal carbon footprint data to track improvement. Through this action, we aim to demonstrate how individual-level energy conservation can make a real impact on achieving SDG 7 (Affordable & Clean Energy) and SDG 13 (Climate Action). At the same time, energy conservation not only helps reduce carbon emissions but also lowers household electricity bills, achieving a win-win of economic and environmental benefits. We believe that as more people begin to pay attention to household energy efficiency and take action, the cumulative effect will make a meaningful contribution to national carbon peaking and carbon neutrality goals.

Step 3: Three Carbon Offset Plans

Option 1

Comprehensive LED Lighting Replacement: Replace all incandescent bulbs and fluorescent lights in the home with high-efficiency LED bulbs. For a typical three-bedroom, two-living-room home with approximately 25 light bulbs, replacing all of them can reduce emissions by approximately 450 kg CO₂ per year. LED bulbs consume only 10-20% of the energy of incandescent bulbs, and have a lifespan of over 25,000 hours. The initial investment is approximately 500 CNY, but annual electricity savings of 600-800 CNY mean the payback period is less than one year. Additionally, LED bulbs do not contain harmful substances such as mercury, and their environmental impact after disposal is far less than that of fluorescent lights.

Option 2

Install Smart Plugs to Eliminate Standby Energy Consumption: Equip commonly used household appliances such as TVs, computerss, air conditioners, and microwaves with smart plugs or smart power strips to completely eliminate standby power consumption through scheduled on/off and remote control functions. Research shows that a household standby energy consumption can reach 300-500 kWh per year, equivalent to approximately 300 kg CO₂ emissions. Using smart plugs (approximately 50-80 CNY each) with mobile app management can reduce standby energy consumption by over 90%. Additionally, develop the habit of turning off lights and unplugging infrequently used electrical appliances to further reduce unnecessary energy waste.

Option 3

Purchase Green Electricity or Install Rooftop Solar: Apply for a green electricity plan from your power company to ensure 100% of household electricity comes from renewable sources such as wind and solar. If conditions permit, install a 3 kW distributed photovoltaic system on your rooftop, generating approximately 3,300 kWh annually, which can cover most household electricity needs and reduce emissions by approximately 1,500 kg CO₂ per year. Many Chinese cities now offer distributed photovoltaic subsidy policies, with installation cost recovery periods of approximately 5-7 years, while solar panels have a lifespan exceeding 25 years. SDG 11 · Green Transportation

Step 1: Purpose of the Action

The transportation sector is the second-largest source of global greenhouse gas emissions, accounting for approximately 24% of total global CO₂ emissions, with road transport (private cars, trucks) contributing nearly three-quarters of the sector emissions. In China, with accelerating urbanization and continuously growing vehicle ownership (exceeding 350 million vehicles nationwide by the end of 2025), carbon emission pressures in the transport sector are increasingly prominent. Comparing the carbon emission intensity of different travel modes: private cars emit approximately 150-200 grams per kilometer per person, buses approximately 30-50 grams, subways approximately 10-20 grams, while walking and cycling produce zero emissions. Yet in urban commuting, private car usage still exceeds 40%. The purpose of this action is to comprehensively record and analyze personal travel data (including commute mode, travel frequency, and mileage), quantify transportation carbon footprint, and test the feasibility and emission reduction effectiveness of replacing private cars with public transport, non-motorized vehicles, and shared mobility. This action directly supports SDG 11 (Sustainable Cities & Communities) objectives regarding universal access to safe, affordable, and sustainable transport systems. Through firsthand experience and data recording, we hope to set an example for friends and colleagues, demonstrating that Green Transportation is not only an environmentally friendly choice but is also often more economical and healthier.

Step 3: Three Carbon Offset Plans

Option 1

Replace Private Car Commute with Subway + Bike-Sharing: Replace the daily 30 km private car commute with a combined "subway + bike-sharing" travel approach. At approximately 180 grams of carbon emissions per kilometer for private cars, 250 days of annual commuting can reduce emissions by approximately 1,350 kg CO₂. Actual testing shows that subway commute time is comparable to or even shorter than driving during peak hours (approximately 45 minutes vs. 50-70 minutes driving), and the monthly average transport cost drops from approximately 1,200 CNY (fuel + parking + maintenance depreciation) to approximately 300 CNY (monthly subway pass + bike-sharing quarterly card), saving nearly 11,000 CNY annually. Using subway commute time for reading or work can also improve time utilization efficiency.

Option 2

Replace Short-Haul Flights with High-Speed Rail: For domestic travel within 800 km, replace flights with high-speed rail. Taking Beijing to Shanghai (approximately 1,200 km) as an example, a one-way flight emits approximately 350-460 kg CO₂, while high-speed rail emits only approximately 30-50 kg — achieving an 85-90% emission reduction. Even for medium-to-long distance routes of 1,200 to 1,500 km, high-speed rail carbon emissions are only 10-15% of those of flying. China has the world most developed high-speed rail network, covering major city clusters, with door-to-door times comparable to flying. Avoiding two short-to-medium-haul flights each year can reduce emissions by approximately 460-690 kg CO₂.

Option 3

Participate in Carbon Offset Tree Planting Projects: Participate in tree planting projects through platforms such as the China Green Foundation or Ant Forest to offset remaining unavoidable carbon emissions. Based on a mature broadleaf tree absorbing approximately 22-30 kg CO₂ per year, planting 200 trees (requiring approximately 1-2 mu of land) can absorb approximately 4,400-6,000 kg CO₂ annually once established, essentially offsetting one people annual emissions. Beyond purchasing carbon offset credits, actively participating in weekend tree planting volunteer activities and promoting urban greening in communities and schools not only sequesters carbon and releases oxygen but also improves local microclimate, protects biodiversity, and enhances community environmental quality.

SDG 2 / SDG 12 · Plant-Based Diet

Step 1: Purpose of the Action

The global food system contributes approximately 26% of anthropogenic greenhouse gas emissions, with livestock farming accounting for a particularly significant share. According to the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO), the carbon footprint of producing 1 kg of beef is approximately 60 kg CO₂ equivalent (including feed cultivation, enteric fermentation methane emissions, transportation, processing, etc.), while the carbon footprint of producing 1 kg of vegetables is only 0.5-2 kg CO₂ — a difference of 30 to 120 times. China is the world largest meat consumer, with annual per capita meat consumption of approximately 70 kg, far exceeding the healthy intake recommended by the Chinese Dietary Guidelines (approximately 40-50 kg/year). A high-meat diet not only exacerbates climate change but is also associated with increased risks of chronic diseases such as cardiovascular disease, diabetes, and certain cancers. The purpose of this action is to gradually adjust dietary structure — transitioning from a high-meat diet toward a plant-based approach with moderate meat intake — reducing food carbon footprint while improving personal health. This action is closely related to SDG 2 (Zero Hunger and Nutrition), SDG 12 (Responsible Consumption & Production), and SDG 13 (Climate Action). By recording a four-week diet log and quantifying the environmental impact of different food choices, we hope to find a sustainable diet model suitable for Chinese dietary habits that can be maintained long-term, and demonstrate to others that eating healthily and eating sustainably can go hand in hand.

Soy protein content is comparable to beef (approximately 35-38 grams of protein per 100 grams), but its carbon footprint is only about 1/30 of that of beef. China has a wealth of traditional plant protein ingredients such as tofu, dried bean curd sticks, and wheat gluten — meat-free days do not mean sacrificing taste or nutrition. Additionally, replacing milk with soy milk as a daily beverage can reduce an additional approximately 0.3 kg CO₂ per cup.

Option 2

Join a Local CSA Organic Farm and Prioritize Seasonal Ingredients: Join a Community Supported Agriculture (CSA) program and order seasonal vegetables and fruits directly from a local organic farm each week. Local, seasonal ingredients avoid the high carbon emissions of greenhouse cultivation and long-distance refrigerated transport, and organic farming does not use synthetic nitrogen fertilizers (nitrogen fertilizer production is one of the main sources of agricultural carbon emissions). At the same time, reducing food miles means the distance from farm to table does not exceed 100 km. It is estimated that purchasing vegetables from a local CSA farm instead of imported supermarket vegetables can reduce emissions by approximately 300 kg CO₂ per year, and the CSA model helps small and medium-sized farmers earn a stable income, promoting local agricultural sustainable development.

Option 3

Balcony Garden + Kitchen Waste Composting: Use balcony or windowsill space to grow easy-to-manage crops such as herbs (basil, mint, cilantro), leafy greens (lettuce, spinach, baby bok choy), and cherry tomatoes. A 3-square-meter balcony garden can produce approximately 30-50 kg of vegetables per year, reducing emissions by approximately 150 kg CO₂ compared to supermarket purchases (including carbon emissions from packaging, transportation, and refrigeration). At the same time, using a small compost bin to convert kitchen waste (fruit peels, vegetable leaves, coffee grounds, etc.) into organic fertilizer reduces methane emissions from landfills while providing free, high-quality fertilizer for the balcony garden, creating a small household material circulation system. The growing process itself is also a great way to relieve stress and connect with nature.

SDG 12 · 4R Circular Economy

Step 1: Purpose of the Action

Globally, approximately 2 billion tons of municipal solid waste are generated each year, of which only about 13.5% is recycled, with the majority being landfilled or incinerated. Landfills are the world third-largest anthropogenic source of methane, and methane greenhouse effect is over 80 times that of CO₂ on a 20-year timescale. In China, fast fashion culture and the rapid iteration of electronic products have led to a sharp increase in consumption waste — per capita, approximately 30 kg of used clothing is discarded each year, and over 10 million tons of electronic waste is generated annually, but the formal recycling rate is less than 40%. Many discarded items still have usable value or can have their lifespan extended through simple repairs. The purpose of this action is to comprehensively practice the 4R principles (Reduce, Reuse, Repair, Recycle) — starting from consumption source reduction, through item reuse and self-repair, and ultimately ensuring that recyclables enter the proper recycling channels. This action directly corresponds to SDG 12 (Responsible Consumption & Production) specific objectives regarding "substantially reducing waste generation through prevention, reduction, recycling, and reuse." Through three months of practice and data recording, we hope to quantify the potential for personal waste reduction and demonstrate that sustainable consumption not only does not lower quality of life but can actually lead to a simpler, more conscious lifestyle.

Results: Personal annual carbon footprint is approximately 5,100 kg CO₂。Consumer goods and waste-related emissions account for 28% (approximately 1,428 kg CO₂), higher than the average resident level, mainly due to frequent purchases of new clothing (monthly average of 3-4 new items) and relatively rapid replacement of electronic products (phone replacement every 1-2 years). Notably, due to insufficiently detailed waste sorting, approximately 60% of recyclables are mixed and discarded, causing a large amount of reusable resources to enter landfill or incineration processes, indirectly increasing carbon emissions. If complete recycling and consumption reduction are achieved, the carbon footprint of the waste sector can be reduced by over 61%.

Step 3: Three Carbon Offset Plans

Option 1

"30-Day No-Shopping Challenge" + Prioritize Second-Hand Purchases: Implement a "30 days of not purchasing non-essential items" challenge — pause all consumption except for food and daily necessities to break the inertia of impulse buying. Afterward, for genuinely needed clothing and household items, prioritize purchasing from second-hand platforms such as Xianyu and Duozhuayu. Purchasing second-hand goods avoids the carbon emissions from producing new products — taking a cotton T-shirt as an example, the production process emits approximately 6-8 kg CO₂, and buying second-hand can avoid approximately 90% of these emissions. If 50% of annual clothing purchases shift to the second-hand market, combined with prioritizing second-hand for larger items (such as furniture, books, and toys), this can reduce emissions by approximately 800 kg CO₂ per year while saving approximately 3,000-5,000 CNY in consumption spending.

Option 2

Electronics Self-Repair + Extended Lifespan: Learn basic electronic product maintenance and repair skills (such as replacing phone batteries, cleaning computer dust, and replacing hard drives), extending phone lifespan from 2 years to 4 years, and laptops from 3 years to 5-6 years. Taking a smartphone as an example, its production phase carbon emissions are approximately 50-85 kg CO₂ (mainly from chip manufacturing and rare metal mining). If the replacement cycle is extended from 2 years to 4 years, annual carbon emissions drop from approximately 30-43 kg to approximately 15-21 kg. Additionally, using online repair guides such as iFixit to self-replace batteries and screens avoids repair costs and reduces demand for new products. Extending a laptop lifespan by 2 years reduces annual carbon emissions by approximately 20-30 kg CO₂.

Option 3

Four-Category Refined Waste Management + Special E-Waste Recycling: Set up four sorting bins at home (recyclables, kitchen waste, other waste, hazardous waste) to ensure each type of waste enters the correct processing channel. Specifically, collect electronic waste such as used batteries, charging cables, and old phones separately, and deliver them to specialized e-waste recycling points (such as Aihuishou, Huawei/Apple store recycling programs). After refined classification, household recyclable recovery rates can increase from below 40% to over 85%, recycling an additional approximately 120 kg of waste paper, plastic, and glass annually, reducing approximately 400 kg CO₂ from landfill or incineration emissions. Additionally, separating kitchen waste for composting or anaerobic digestion can avoid methane production in landfills, reducing approximately 100-150 kg CO₂ equivalent in greenhouse gas emissions per year.
